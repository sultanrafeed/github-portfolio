02 Position

The argument I am making

Using a language model to score another language model was adopted faster than it was validated. It holds up worst exactly where the stakes are highest: outside English, on items where human annotators genuinely disagree, and in retrieval pipelines that ought to abstain rather than answer. My work sits in those three places, and the resources needed to study them mostly do not exist yet, so I build themE05.

That is not a position I arrived at from the literature. It came out of my own results. On LegalRAG the failure mode no configuration I tested could resolve was the out-of-context and unanswerable queryE04. On DFAR the interesting number was not accuracy but the gap between a correct label and correct reasoning, which is why we had to invent a metric for itE01. In my own judge study, four open judges agreed on the ranking and disagreed wildly on reliabilityE06.

Three directions I want to push on

  1. D1 Judge reliability outside English Measure rather than assume judge reliability in Bangla and comparable Global South languages. Treat human preference as distributional rather than as a single consensus point, and test whether rubric redesign, reference grounding or judge ensembling actually recovers agreement, or whether reliability degrades specifically on culturally situated items.E06E04
  2. D2 Whether safety alignment transfers across languages Attack success and defence robustness vary sharply by language, and safety tuning done in English does not reliably carry into low-resource, non-Latin-script settings. Build refusal and red-team resources for Bangla with human-written rationales, then test whether alignment transfers by parameter-space methods (safety-vector merging, layer-wise swapping, self-distillation) rather than full retraining.E01E03
  3. D3 Faithfulness and abstention in agentic retrieval Diagnose component by component where in a retrieval and reasoning pipeline faithfulness breaks, on local open models rather than proprietary ones, with out-of-context handling treated as a primary metric instead of an error bucket.E04E08

03 Research

Four papers, and what each one actually showed

All four have completed peer review and are published at the venue named. The arXiv identifiers point to author preprints of the same accepted papers, not to separate submissions. Open an exhibit for the numbers.

  1. A model can label an action unethical while reasoning nothing like a person. Does supervising the label alone get you anything? We built DFAR, a 5,000 statement ethics dataset carrying human-written rationales alongside labels, and proposed the Misalignment Rate, a metric that separates a correct label from correct reasoning. Reason-supervised fine-tuning took Llama-2 7B from 36.4% to 89.4% accuracy and cut Misalignment Rate from 52.0% to 9.4%.E01 ICPR 2024 · second author
  2. What breaks in retrieval when the corpus is legal, the language is low-resource, and the query is phrased the way a person actually types it? LegalRAG added a relevance check and a query refinement stage over vanilla RAG for bilingual Bangla and English legal documents, lifting cosine similarity from 0.76 to 0.82 and human evaluation from 3.41 to 3.70 out of 5. The out-of-context and unanswerable query survived every configuration we tried, which is the finding that set my research agenda.E04 IJCNN 2025 · equal first author
  3. Can a language model take on the slow middle of a meta-analysis without quietly inventing the findings it is meant to be summarising? We built MAD (625 meta-articles over 6,344 supporting abstracts) and introduced Inverse Cosine Distance, a loss for long-context fine-tuning. A thirteen annotator evaluation returned 87.6% relevance, with irrelevant output falling from 4.56% to 1.9%.E02 IEEE Big Data 2024 · second author
  4. How much diagnostic accuracy survives when the model has to fit on hardware a clinic can actually afford? Multi-teacher distillation into a 0.35M parameter student, 82 times smaller than DenseNet161, reaching 88.0% on HAM10000 at 1.05M parameters through model souping and ensembling. I include it as evidence of compression and model merging technique, which I want to point at cross-lingual safety transfer, not as a separate research programme.E03 Intelligence-Based Medicine · Q1 journal

    BanglaEval, an evaluation harness that reports its own disagreement

    Problem
    Multilingual leaderboards publish a single averaged score. In a low-resource language the disagreement behind that score is not noise around the result. It is a large part of the result.
    What it does
    Three tracks. Instruction penalises drift into English, which naive scoring rewards. Factuality tags unanswerable items so that abstention is rewarded and confident invention is punished. Refusal checks that harmful requests are declined clearly and in Bangla, without rewarding over-refusal of the benign part of a request.
    Design
    Every judge scores every response, so judge disagreement is measurable rather than averaged away, and judges emit a rationale before a score. The leaderboard reports judge spread, the gap between the most and least generous judge, as a column. Stages are append-only and resumable; backends sit behind one contract so adding a provider is a config entry, not a code change.
    Honesty
    The repository ships a LIMITATIONS file. Small judge panels have correlated blind spots, judges reward verbosity, a judge sharing a family with a candidate may favour it, and the human calibration subset is small.

    Cross-model LLM-as-judge evaluation

    Setup
    Four small open judges (Phi-3 Mini 3.8B, StableLM Zephyr 3B, Gemma 2B, GPT-2) scored 500 statement and reason pairs on a six-point rubric across six response systems (Llama-2 7B and Mistral 7B, each pre-trained, label-tuned and reason-tuned), benchmarked against three-evaluator majority-vote human labels rather than against a single reference model.
    Finding
    Judge scores recovered the human ranking of systems at Spearman rho of −0.60 against Misalignment Rate, negative by construction. But agreement was heavily judge-dependent, from −0.71 (Phi-3 Mini, Gemma 2B) down to −0.09 (Zephyr 3B). Consistent judge rankings did not imply consistent judge reliability.
    The interesting failure
    A rank inversion: the worst system by human evaluation (Llama-2 7B pre-trained, 55.0% Misalignment Rate) placed near the top by judge score. And a degenerate judge, GPT-2, scoring a mean of 1.21 to 2.73 against 2.83 to 3.56 for Phi-3 Mini. Judge capability, not judge count, bounds panel reliability.
